## Appendix slides (hidden, pull up if asked)

### A1 — Foundational research
UXR study, 11 participants, 13–24, split regular/non-regular game players. Three findings: mental-model split, expected games in chat, wanted to play with friends.

### A2 — Live multiplayer framework
Three rules: always start solo, drop in from chat, stay warm until empty. Blockbuster as in-house flagship. Turn-based games moved onto the same framework.

### A3 — Daily Challenges (the misread, candidly)
Scoped as solo retention. Win landed in **group sends** (+1.8%), group views, +3.89M reciprocal friendships. Players were using single-player puzzles as a reason to talk to each other. Changed the leaderboards roadmap.

### A4 — Discover carousel tradeoff
Three options pressure-tested: Tile (sells one game), Carousel (catalog merchandising), Pill (overlooked). Carousel won — discoverability, feed citizenship, storefront equity, ad inventory.

### A5 — Spotlight Games tab
10.0% view-to-unlock vs 0.6% on For You vs 0.3% on Following. 7.6× share rate. 2× lens unlocks. +12% session depth.

## Stress-test answer

### "A decision where data and judgment disagreed?"

> Data said hyper-casual was Snap's library and "monetize what we have" was the obvious move — that was my first pitch. Judgment, after studying the market, said the library itself was the wrong base. Hyper-casual structurally caps out on IAP. The growth is in hybrid-casual. I killed my own pitch and went back with the category bet. That's the call that earned the squad.
