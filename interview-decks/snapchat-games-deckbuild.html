Snapchat Games — second case

Universal second case after Topic Chats. No standalone framing slide, the deck-level framing belongs to the anchor. Toggle the role pill above to swap the handoff line, key tie-back notes, and the close. All assets pulled from public/projects/snapchat-games/.

Handoff line — say before slide 1
"The second case is shorter and sits in a different domain, but the through-line is the same. I want to show you how I treated games not as entertainment, but as a community feature. As a reason for friends to talk."
"The second case is shorter, a different product but the same pattern. I argued an entire content category into existence inside an app that didn't have one, and then built the distribution system to support it."
"The second case is shorter, a different product but the growth pattern is the one I want to land. Spotting an unowned signal in the data, shipping the loops, and earning the bigger bet from the results."
Slide design system — template-aligned, with deliberate breaks
Baseline (the template)
Full aurora background. Eyebrow top-left in mono caps, page number bottom-right (mono). Title in Instrument Serif — italic for hero/single-word/big-metric moments, roman for declarative two-line headlines. Body in Geist sans. Phones orthogonal in device frames; annotations via thin gray leader lines.
Typography hierarchy
Italic serif — covers, single-word titles, big metric numerals ($100M, 47%), one-liners where rhythm matters. Roman serif — declarative 2-line headlines. Bold sans — alternate headline when slide already has a strong italic element below (metric grids, diagrams). Mono caps — eyebrows, page numbers, annotations, axis labels, attributions.
Visual treatment
Phones orthogonal by default (template style), device-framed. Bare screenshots in soft surface cards for data viz. Tilts only on break slides or scrapbook moments — 2–4° max. Leader lines hairline gray. Soft pastel fills for diagram shapes (peach/lavender/blue). Mono LABEL inside shapes.
Color
Aurora bg every content slide. One filled pill / accent per slide max. The kill slide is the only deliberate dark-surface — and it should look like a kill, not template chrome.
⌖ Deliberate template breaks (3 max per deck)
Slide 17 · Evan quote
Kill the left hairline rule. Italic display serif fills 80% of canvas. "go big" set in upright (un-italic) — visual inversion makes the CEO's ask land as a directive. Mono attribution bottom-right, small. Staff signal: hold a room with one sentence.
Slide 18 · The kill
This must not look like a template slide. Strike "Arcade economy..." in faded neutral italic, small, top-left. Drop "Hyper-casual caps out on IAP" at 1.5× deck headline scale, bottom-left, rest of canvas empty. Asymmetry is the point. Staff signal: she killed her own idea and is showing the receipt.
Slide 22 · 6 surfaces
Staggered with visible SOCIAL / PASSIVE lane labels in mono caps at the left margin. One phone rotated ~4° off-axis as the disruptive Resurrection bet. Staff signal: systems thinking with the bets named.
Three mistakes to avoid when breaking template
1. Breaking more than 3 slides — if everything's hero, nothing is.   2. Breaking for decoration, not argument — every break is tied to a beat. Never tilt for cool.   3. Half-committing — if the numeral bleeds, bleed it 15–20%, not 3%. Timid breaks read as mistakes.

Slide 11 · ~30s · BET 2 · SOLUTION
Bet 2, the solution
On-slide text
Fullscreen on start. One unified capture: tap = silent start. Long-press = start + hands-free recording. Round auto-progresses to Preview.
Talk track
"I redesigned the game lens experience to open fullscreen on start, and unified the capture control. A single tap starts the game silently. Fading the camera UI after gameplay starts meant users could play without fear of accidentally tapping camera controls, which is what unlocked more complicated mechanics on the platform."

Stress-test cheat sheet
"A decision where data and judgment disagreed?"
Data said hyper-casual was Snap's library and "monetize what we have" was the obvious move — that was my first pitch. Judgment, after studying the market, said the library itself was the wrong base. Hyper-casual structurally caps out on IAP. The growth is in hybrid-casual. I killed my own pitch and went back with the category bet. That's the call that earned the squad.
"You killed the arcade economy after Evan greenlit it. Walk me through the conversation where you told him you were throwing out the pitch he'd backed — what did you actually say, and what would you have done if he'd told you to ship it anyway?"
⚠ Prep a real answer. HM-flagged as the hardest games panel question. The current talk track ("I stopped pitching, killed my own deck, spent two weeks studying the market") frames the kill as a clean intellectual pivot. A panel will want the messier version. Four things to prep:

1. Who you told first — your manager, the squad lead, the GM, or Evan directly? Did engineering already have story points loaded? What did the eng lead think?
2. What you actually said to Evan — verbatim opening, not the conclusion. ("I want to walk back the arcade pitch" vs "I've been studying the market and I think we got the category wrong.")
3. What you brought with you — the chart, the competitor breakdown, the IAP-ceiling analysis, the hybrid-casual revenue data. What was the artifact that made the new direction defensible.
4. Plan B — what you would have done if Evan had said "I hear you, ship arcade anyway." Would you have shipped it and continued making the category case? Resigned the squad lead role? Pushed harder? This is the question the answer turns on. A staff candidate has a real answer here.
Tone: re-enact the conversation, don't summarize the conclusion. "I went to [name] first because [reason]. I opened with [specific phrase] because I needed [their reaction]. If he'd pushed back, I would have…" If you can answer this crisply, the kill becomes the strongest beat in any case you present. If you can't, the kill reads as designer-says-no rather than staff-leads-the-room.
