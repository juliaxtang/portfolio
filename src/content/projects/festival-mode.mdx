<Section size="sub" stage="Background" title="Festival Mode in 2019" hideFromNav class="cs-festival-2019">
Festival Mode first launched in 2019: a single-festival entry that let
users opt in to swipe on people going to the same event. It got pulled
when festivals were cancelled during the pandemic. With IRL events
returning in 2022, Tinder wanted to bring it back and refresh it for the
way the app had evolved.
</Section>

Festival Mode tile could give the page the specificity it was missing.

<Section size="sub" stage="Ideate" title="Narrowing the MVP" hideFromNav>
Two months meant ruthless scoping. After early reviews with engineers, I
deferred richer features (RSVPs, event-specific prompts) to future
milestones. The MVP scope landed on three things.
</Section>

  <Callout number="2" title="Opt in and out">
    Users can change their mind any time. Opt-out is no longer hidden.
  </Callout>
  <Callout number="3" title="Festival-curated swiping">

<Section size="sub" stage="Test" title="Single-select or multi-select?" hideFromNav>
The biggest open UX question was whether festivalgoers should pick one
festival or multiple. I had two hypotheses.

- **H1.** Multi-select preferred, since many haven't decided on a single festival yet.
- **H2.** Festivalgoers may want richer features (cross-festival messaging, etc.), but are okay with using messaging to accomplish the same in MVP.

I tested two flows: single-select with separate cardstacks per festival,
versus multi-select with one mixed cardstack.
</Section>

<Section size="sub" stage="Test" title="UXR Results" hideFromNav>
The MVP met festivalgoers' needs. Across 12 participants:

- **10 of 12** said they'd use it. All described it as intuitive and easy to use.
- **Multi-select preferred** for opt-in flexibility, since most hadn't decided on a single festival yet.
- **Separated stacks felt more organized** during the actual swiping. The single mixed stack led to incorrect swipes because users couldn't tell which festival a person was from.
- **Need for catch-all participation.** Due to partnership constraints, some users didn't see their festival listed but still wanted to participate.

<Quote>
I like to get to know people a bit before meeting up with them. This
feature helps me to do that.
</Quote>

<Quote>
I would 10/10 use this. This would be so helpful in finding people to
go with.
</Quote>
</Section>

<Section size="sub" stage="Test" title="The combination flow" hideFromNav>
Neither flow won outright, so I made the call to combine the strongest
parts of each: the opt-in pattern from Flow 2, the swiping pattern from
Flow 1, plus a generic tile to solve the catch-all problem.
</Section>

<Section stage="Results" eyebrow="Results" title="What launched, and what it moved" banner />

<Section size="sub" stage="Results" hideFromNav>
Users were more engaged in Festival Mode than they were in the main
Tinder experience. The generic Festivalgoers tile meaningfully expanded
the audience without diluting the core experience. Each festival got its
own visual treatment, branded with the marketing team and built from
existing design system patterns, so partners felt distinct without a
custom build per event.
</Section>

<Section size="sub" stage="Results" title="What I'd carry forward" hideFromNav>
The biggest lesson was that opt-in friction is a feature, not a bug, when
the goal is purposeful connection. The biggest thing I'd do over: kill
the second opt-in step. I saw the drop-off in the data and I'd push
harder next time to fold festival selection into the first tap.
</Section>
