<Section stage="Overview" eyebrow="Overview" title="A seasonal experience on Explore">
A new experience on Tinder Explore that lets festivalgoers meet others
going to the same festival. The team relaunched and refreshed Festival
Mode in just two months for the return of IRL events, and the experience
went on to outperform the main Tinder swipe deck on both engagement and
conversation rate.

### Role
Product Design Lead, partnering with a Product Manager, a team of
engineers, a Data Scientist, a UX Researcher, and the Marketing and
Creative teams.

### Duration
Two months from kickoff to launch.
</Section>

<Section stage="Define" eyebrow="Define" title="Why not just relaunch the 2019 version?">
Festival Mode initially launched in 2019, but got pulled when festivals
were cancelled during the pandemic. With the return of IRL events, Tinder
wanted to relaunch it. The 2019 version had real limitations, mostly
because it didn't have a permanent home in the app.
</Section>

<Callouts cols={3} wide>
  <Callout eyebrow="Limitation 1" title="One-time entry point" tone="outline">
    Limited flexibility. Could only run one festival at a time, which
    capped partnership opportunities.
  </Callout>
  <Callout eyebrow="Limitation 2" title="Forced decisions" tone="outline">
    Users had to decide there and then if they were interested. The
    opt-out was hidden.
  </Callout>
  <Callout eyebrow="Limitation 3" title="Lack of curation" tone="outline">
    Positioning on the main page meant no way to guarantee you'd only
    see people from your festival.
  </Callout>
</Callouts>

<Section stage="Ideate" eyebrow="Ideate" title="Picking a layout for the Explore entry">
Explore was a new tab in the app with experiences that helped people
connect in fun, focused ways. Festival Mode would be one of those
experiences, so we needed to figure out how it should appear on the
Explore page.

We ran a multivariate layout test against six candidates: a carousel, a
2x8 grid, a horizontal ticker, a vertical scroll, vertical tiles, and a
hero tile.

- The **carousel** and **2x8 grid** got cut early. In concept testing, users were overwhelmed with the amount of content.
- The **horizontal ticker** and **vertical scroll** were tested as part of broader Explore testing and didn't perform.
- The **hero tile won** on both swipes sent and tile opens. We went with that.
</Section>

<Section stage="Ideate" eyebrow="Ideate" title="Narrowing the MVP">
Two months meant ruthless scoping. After early reviews with engineers, we
deferred richer features (RSVPs, event-specific prompts) to future
milestones. The MVP scope landed on three things.
</Section>

<Callouts cols={3} wide>
  <Callout number="1" title="Multiple festival support">
    The core differentiator from 2019. I focused most of my UX work here.
  </Callout>
  <Callout number="2" title="Opt in and out">
    Users can change their mind any time. Opt-out is no longer hidden.
  </Callout>
  <Callout number="3" title="Festival-curated swiping">
    See and swipe on profiles who have also opted in to a festival.
  </Callout>
</Callouts>

<Section stage="Test" eyebrow="Test" title="Single-select or multi-select?">
The biggest open UX question was whether festivalgoers should pick one
festival or multiple. We had two hypotheses.

- **H1.** Multi-select preferred, since many haven't decided on a single festival yet.
- **H2.** Festivalgoers may want richer features (cross-festival messaging, etc.), but are okay with using messaging to accomplish the same in MVP.

We tested two flows: single-select with separate cardstacks per festival,
versus multi-select with one mixed cardstack.
</Section>

<Section stage="Test" eyebrow="Test" title="Results">
The MVP met festivalgoers' needs. Across 12 participants:

- **10 of 12** said they'd use it. All described it as intuitive and easy to use.
- **Multi-select preferred** for opt-in flexibility, since most hadn't decided on a single festival yet.
- **Separated stacks felt more organized** during the actual swiping. The single mixed stack led to incorrect swipes because users couldn't tell which festival a person was from.
- **Need for catch-all participation.** Due to partnership constraints, some users didn't see their festival listed but still wanted to participate.
</Section>

<Quote>
I like to get to know people a bit before meeting up with them. This
feature helps me to do that.
</Quote>

<Section stage="Refine" eyebrow="Refine" title="The combination flow">
The final UX combined what worked from each test condition into one flow.
</Section>

<Callouts cols={3} wide>
  <Callout number="1" title="Multi-select during opt-in">
    Lets users pick more than one festival up front, since most hadn't
    decided on just one.
  </Callout>
  <Callout number="2" title="Separated cardstacks">
    Clearer delineation while swiping. No mixed-festival confusion or
    incorrect swipes.
  </Callout>
  <Callout number="3" title='Generic "Festivalgoers" tile'>
    People whose festival isn't listed can still participate and meet
    other festivalgoers.
  </Callout>
</Callouts>

<Section stage="Impact" eyebrow="Looking ahead" title="Influence on the Explore roadmap">
Festival Mode's seasonal success directly informed Explore's future
roadmap.
</Section>

<Callouts cols={3} wide>
  <Callout number="1" title="Event-based dating" tone="outline">
    Users want offline connections, but they look to Explore for
    specificity and curation. We started exploring event-based experiences
    that connect people through shared interests beyond music festivals.
  </Callout>
  <Callout number="2" title="Opt-in flows for purposeful connection" tone="outline">
    Introducing a healthy amount of friction fosters more purposeful user
    connections, even when it costs you some volume.
  </Callout>
  <Callout number="3" title="Reducing first-time drop-off" tone="outline">
    Minimize the user drop-off we saw by removing the additional opt-in
    step and giving users a faster way to select the festivals they're
    interested in.
  </Callout>
</Callouts>

<Section stage="Impact" title="Future milestones we sketched">
- **RSVP to events.** "Interested" or "Going" signals to let other users know intent.
- **Event-based prompts.** Use our existing Spotify integration to let users add the top artists they want to see at a specific festival.
</Section>
