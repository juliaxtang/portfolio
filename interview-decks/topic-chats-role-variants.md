# Topic Chats — role variants

The Topic Chats anchor deck is universal. **Only these things change per role:**

1. Slide 1 (framing) — different content per role
2. A few key speaker-note tweaks on slides 12, 14, 18, 20 (the "tie-back to your world" lines)
3. The close

Build slide 1 three times in Figma (duplicated frames, hide the two you're not using before each call). Speaker note diffs are below — paste the role version into Figma Slides presenter notes.

---

## Patreon — community / organic discovery

### Slide 1 — Framing
**On-slide text** (3 short lines, generous spacing):
> Staff designer, Snap
>
> I take ambiguous, cross-surface problems and reframe them before scoping
>
> Today: two products where community engagement was stuck until I found the unlock

**Talk track (~60s):**
"Quick framing. I'm a staff product designer at Snap. The kinds of problems I take are ambiguous, cross-surface, exec-visible — and the way I tend to work is to reframe the problem before scoping the solution. The two cases I want to walk through both share that pattern: community engagement that was stuck until I found a wedge to design against. The first one is a public-conversation product that looked active but wasn't — and the real problem turned out to be the one nobody on the team was naming."

### Tie-back speaker notes (paste into Figma)

- **Slide 12** ("One signal, five surfaces"): add line: *"For Patreon, this is the question of whether 'creator is live' or 'new drop' is a first-class signal across the app, not a notification you catch once."*
- **Slide 14** (entry point constraint): add line: *"This is the move I want to land for Patreon — constraint-aware growth design. Don't fight for prime placement until the product has earned it; in the meantime build the loops that fire at the moment users have the highest intent."*
- **Slide 20** (close): add line: *"The takeaway I'd carry to Patreon: communities scale when you stop trying to be everything at once and pick the wedge where the audience already wants to be in the same place at the same time."*

## Pinterest — discovery / social experiences

### Slide 1 — Framing
**On-slide text:**
> Staff designer, Snap
>
> I build discovery as a system across surfaces, not a single destination
>
> Today: two products where the design problem was distribution, not the home screen

**Talk track (~60s):**
"Quick framing. I'm a staff product designer at Snap. The work I'm most known for is treating discovery as a *system* — content meets users where they already are, across multiple surfaces, with a shared signal — rather than fighting for one home. The two cases I want to walk through both share that pattern. The first is a public-conversation product where I solved discovery by making 'live' a property the whole app could speak."

### Tie-back speaker notes

- **Slide 12** ("One signal, five surfaces"): **this is your hero slide for Pinterest** — dwell longer. Add: *"This is the part I most want to talk about. Discovery is multi-surface or it isn't discovery. For Pinterest, this maps directly to how a single signal — trending, fresh, in-progress, picked-for-you — travels across home, search, board, notifications, without becoming five different things."*
- **Slide 14** (entry point constraint): add: *"Constraint-aware discovery design — when the front door isn't available, the system has to do the work."*
- **Slide 20** (close): add: *"Two takeaways for Pinterest: one, discovery has to travel; two, retention is a property of the system, not of any one surface."*

## Reddit — Growth, retention from SEO landings

### Slide 1 — Framing
**On-slide text:**
> Staff designer, Snap
>
> I solve retention by finding the loop that brings people back, not just the moment that brings them in
>
> Today: two products where I built the re-entry mechanism from scratch

**Talk track (~60s):**
"Quick framing. I'm a staff product designer at Snap. Growth is loops, not landings — the work I want to walk through is one where the inherited product was great at landings and terrible at loops. One-and-done participation, no signal pulling users back. The shape of that problem is the same one Reddit faces when users land cold from Google search: the room is empty, there's no reason to stay. The first case is how I fixed it."

### Tie-back speaker notes

- **Slide 9** (scoreboard): add: *"For Reddit, the analog is making a thread feel like it's still happening even when it's old — live counts, momentum signal, an AI summary of what people are talking about right now."*
- **Slide 12** ("One signal, five surfaces"): add: *"Retention is multi-surface or it isn't retention. If the only place a user knows the chat is live is by being in the chat, you lose them. For Reddit this maps to home feed, search results, push, and the iOS lock screen — every place a user lands has to be able to signal 'this thread is hot.'"*
- **Slide 14** (entry point constraint): **this is the highest-value slide for Reddit Growth** — dwell. Add: *"This is the most Reddit-Growth-relevant part of the case. When you can't own the front door, you build the loops that fire at the moment users have the highest intent."*
- **Slide 18** (+32K DAU): add: *"For Reddit Growth this is directly transferable — notification taxonomy is one of the highest-leverage tools for SEO-landed users who haven't opted in to anything specific yet."*
- **Slide 20** (close): add: *"The summary: a one-and-done surface, the wedge with natural concurrency, the live signal across the app, the notification system that pulled users back. Every part of that maps to retention from cold landings."*
