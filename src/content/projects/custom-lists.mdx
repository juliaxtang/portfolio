<Section stage="Overview" eyebrow="Overview" title="Tracking, discovering, and curating">
TV Time is a tracking app for movies and TV, available on iOS, Android, and web,
with over 16 million users globally. People use TV Time to track shows and
movies they love, discover what to watch next, and get notified when new
content is available.

Custom Lists is the feature that lets users curate their own collections of
shows and movies. The team needed a redesign that would make Custom Lists
easier to use, more personal, and shareable, with two clear goals: increase
user engagement with more lists created and shared, and drive organic growth
through list sharing.

### Role
One of two UX designers on this project, end-to-end from research and
ideation through user testing and engineering handoff. We worked
cross-functionally with Product and Development.
</Section>

<Section stage="Define" eyebrow="Framing the problem" title="What users were struggling with">
The UX team had previously run internal user testing on the existing version
of Custom Lists. We revisited the findings and surfaced two main pain points:
a confusing information architecture, and a clunky flow for adding shows to a list.
</Section>

<Section stage="Define" eyebrow="Pain points" title="Confusing information architecture">
Users couldn't find the controls they expected.

- **75%** of participants expected to be able to create a new Custom List from the main Custom Lists screen.
- **All** participants found the location of certain functions (renaming a list, editing the contents of a list) was not where they expected it to be.

### Difficulty adding shows to a list
Editing a list felt clunky and offered no clear feedback.

- **50%** of participants wanted to add shows they weren't already following.
- **75%** were confused by the lack of feedback when adding a show.
- **All** participants found it difficult to navigate the list of shows to add, with no sorting or filtering options.

This testing predated the introduction of Movies, so the redesign also had to account for both shows and movies.
</Section>

<Section stage="Research" eyebrow="Discovery" title="Learning what users want">
We also wanted to understand how to make Custom Lists into a feature more
people would actually want to use. To answer that, we ran internal interviews
with test participants.

The strongest themes:

- Users were drawn to the **social** aspect of Custom Lists.
- Users wanted **more personalization** options for their lists.

Specific features participants asked for: the ability to create a private
list, the option to add a description to a list, and the ability to copy
other people's lists.
</Section>

<Section stage="Ideate" eyebrow="Ideation" title="Mapping the existing flow, sketching a new one">
Since confusing IA was the headline pain point, we mapped the existing user
flow first to visualize problem areas and identify opportunities. Then we

<Section stage="Test" eyebrow="Testing and iterating" title="Round 1: validating the new flow">
I built an InVision prototype from the wireframes and ran a first round of
user testing. We were primarily checking that the updated information
architecture and user flows felt intuitive.

### What worked
- **75%** of participants liked the new personalization options (descriptions, public/private toggle, sorting and filtering).
- **All** participants easily found and used the functions that previously sat in unexpected locations: creating a list, renaming a list, editing list contents.

### What needed work
- **75%** of participants were confused by what some list reordering options meant ("shortest/longest duration").
- **75%** wanted to be able to reorder lists in ascending or descending order.
</Section>

<Section stage="Design" eyebrow="Hi-fidelity" title="Translating to high-fidelity flows">
Once the new flow was validated, I translated the mid-fidelity screens into
detailed, high-fidelity designs, iterating based on the round-one feedback.
The hi-fi screens were arranged into the same two flows.

<Section stage="Test" eyebrow="Round 2" title="Validating the high-fidelity prototype">
I built a hi-fi InVision prototype for a second round of remote user testing.
We had originally planned to run it in person, but the pandemic hit and we
pivoted to remote, with participants interacting via a desktop browser
rather than our test phones. We kept that detail in mind when interpreting
results.

### IA stayed intuitive
**All** participants were still able to easily find and successfully use
Custom List functions, describing the experience as "easy to use,"
"intuitive," and "straightforward."

### Updated functionality landed well
- **All** participants reordered lists with minimal confusion, and appreciated the ascending/descending toggle.
- **75%** liked the new ability to filter list content, which we added to make longer lists easier to parse.

### A larger discoverability issue surfaced
All participants mentioned that Custom Lists were hard to find and felt
hidden in the app. Since this was tied to overall app structure and
navigation, we treated it as out of scope for this project but flagged it
for any future restructuring.

To address it tactically, we designed an in-app announcement to introduce
users to the new Custom Lists, with a CTA to navigate them straight in.
</Section>

<Section stage="Design" eyebrow="Final designs" title="Introducing: new Custom Lists">
After one last round of design clean-up, we shipped a Custom Lists that
addressed both user pain points and user wants, while moving the business
goals forward.

### What's new
- More intuitive information architecture
- More control and clarity when editing list contents (add content you're not following, feedback when adding/removing, sorting and filtering)
- Social functionality: sharing lists, copying others' lists
- Personalization: private lists, list descriptions
</Section>

<Section stage="Impact" eyebrow="Outcomes" title="Did this work?" wide>
After launch, we ran an A/B test on the new version. The metrics moved
in the right direction across the board: more new users created Custom
Lists, more lists were created overall, and existing users engaged with
the feature more too.
