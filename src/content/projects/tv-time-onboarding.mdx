<Section stage="Overview" eyebrow="Overview" title="Rethinking onboarding in a week">
TV Time is a tracking app for movies and TV with over 16 million users. To
explore how onboarding could better teach the app's key function, key
benefits, and core habit loop, the UX team ran a week-long Design Sprint,
ending Friday with a tested prototype.

### Role
I participated in the Sprint end-to-end, from Day 1 (framing the problem)
to Day 5 (testing the solution). I worked closely with the Director of UX
who facilitated, plus the rest of the UX team and Product. My idea was
chosen during the Sprint, so I led wireframing, design, and prototyping
the solution we tested.
</Section>

<Section stage="Research" eyebrow="Pre-sprint research" title="Mobile app onboarding">
I read up on the three high-level types of mobile onboarding so we'd have
shared vocabulary going into the Sprint:

- **Benefits-oriented onboarding** describes the benefits of using the app and how to take advantage of them.
- **Function-oriented onboarding** specifies the main functionality of the app, when you can use it and how. It focuses on helping the user get started.
- **Progressive onboarding** is delivered during actual app usage, on the assumption that users learn more by doing.

Common techniques include inline hinting, dismissible tooltips, slides,
wizards, step-by-step tutorials, and hybrids of the above.
</Section>

<Section stage="Define" eyebrow="Sprint Day 1" title="Framing the problem">
Day 1 was about mapping the challenge. We started at the end by agreeing
on the long-term goal:

> All users come away from their first session understanding key function,
> key benefits, and core habits.

We then identified three core TV Time user types using prior research:

- **Trackers** — predominantly use TV Time to keep track of shows and movies.
- **Discoverers** — predominantly use TV Time to find new content to watch.
- **Watchers** — think they can watch shows and movies *inside* TV Time.

We pinned the completed goal as "second session return," then drew the
paths between each user type and that goal to produce a map.
</Section>

<Section stage="Define" eyebrow="Day 1 (cont)" title="How might we…">
The next exercise was reframing potential issues as opportunities on
sticky notes, organizing them, and voting. From the wall of notes, we
landed on four to focus on:

- How might we determine user intent and personalize the onboarding flow to that intent?
- How might we better align onboarding with the actual app?
- How might we define and narrow the key behavior, the core habit loop we want to teach?
- How might we make it feel rewarding right away?
</Section>

<Section stage="Ideate" eyebrow="Sprint Day 3" title="Personalized onboarding">
My idea was one of two we decided to move forward with. The thesis was
simple: provide a personalized onboarding experience depending on the
type of user.

A user taps the CTA on the registration screen tied to the value prop
that's most appealing to them. The onboarding that follows is tailored to
that value prop. Once they're done, they land in a part of the app that's
most relevant to their user type.

### Why this approach
- It addresses the HMWs around personalized intent and narrowing the core habit loop.
- It provides value for *all* user types, not just Trackers.
- It teaches users the value of tracking in terms of their specific use case, framed as a core habit loop.

### Things to keep an eye on
- Users may not get a chance to learn about other parts of the app, since onboarding is targeted.
- Users may tap the first CTA they see rather than choosing intentionally, so we needed to prioritize the screens (Trackers first, since they're the top user type).
</Section>

<Section stage="Test" eyebrow="Sprint Day 5" title="Testing the prototype">
We counterbalanced the prototypes with sessions in the actual app to see
whether users could understand the real app better after interacting with
our onboarding prototypes. We made sure to recruit new users who weren't
already familiar with TV Time.

### What worked
- **All** participants understood the **key functions** of the app by the end of the session.
- **All** participants understood the **key benefits** of the app by the end of the session.
- **50%** of participants were familiar with **tracking** (our core habit loop) by the end. All participants came away knowing how to search and add shows.

After interacting with the prototype, all participants understood how to
search and add shows in the real app, and 50% immediately and easily knew
how to accomplish other tasks in the real app. One participant noted that
mine had more guidance and explained the app better than the alternative.
</Section>

<Section stage="Test" title="Areas of concern flagged in testing">
- **75%** of participants came away thinking you could watch shows or movies directly inside the app, despite messaging on the welcome screens saying otherwise. (All participants who took the Watcher flow came away understanding you couldn't watch in-app, but the app could direct you to where you could watch.)
- **75%** missed the swipe interaction on the welcome screen that let them view the different CTAs. We had suspected this, which is why the Tracker flow led. We added an auto-scroll to the welcome screens to make swipe more discoverable.
</Section>
