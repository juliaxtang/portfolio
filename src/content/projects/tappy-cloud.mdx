<Section stage="Overview" eyebrow="Overview" title="Tappy, but smarter">
On Tinder, you tap left or right on a profile to flip through profile
"pages," each combining a photo with profile content. Internally, we call
those tappable photo+content pages **Tappy**. Tappy was static, hardcoded,
and hard to scale, so I led a six-month effort to turn it into a
backend-driven, dynamically configurable system, with the tooling to match.

### Role
Product Design Lead, partnering with a Product Manager, a team of engineers,
the Data Science team, the UX Research team, the Design Systems team, and
adjacent teams across every company vertical.

### Duration
Six months from kickoff to launch of the centralized tooling, with rollout
and integration continuing afterwards.
</Section>

<Section stage="Define" eyebrow="Define" title="What was wrong with Tappy">
At the time, Tappy suffered from three structural problems that limited
both what users saw and what teams could ship.
</Section>

<Callouts cols={3} wide>
  <Callout eyebrow="Problem 1" title="Static profiles" tone="outline">
    The ordering of Tappy elements was hardcoded with a one-size-fits-all
    approach. Every user saw the same content in the same order.
  </Callout>
  <Callout eyebrow="Problem 2" title="No UI standardization" tone="outline">
    Profile content looked visually disjointed because there was no shared
    library of profile content components.
  </Callout>
  <Callout eyebrow="Problem 3" title="No centralized control" tone="outline">
    No way to preview different content combinations or manage concurrent
    Tappy experiments, leading to content collisions between teams.
  </Callout>
</Callouts>

<Section stage="Define" eyebrow="Project goals" title="Three goals, one system">
The redesign had to move three things forward at once. Each goal mapped
cleanly to one of the structural problems above, which is how we knew the
solution had to be a system, not a feature.
</Section>

<Callouts cols={3} wide>
  <Callout number="1" title="Business" tone="accent">
    Create a centralized way for teams to preview and manage different
    Tappy content orderings.
  </Callout>
  <Callout number="2" title="User" tone="accent">
    Allow dynamic content ordering so we could surface the most relevant
    content to any given user.
  </Callout>
  <Callout number="3" title="Product" tone="accent">
    Establish UI guidelines for consistency, scalability, and reduced
    visual clutter.
  </Callout>
</Callouts>

<Section stage="Research" eyebrow="Research" title="Where attention actually lands">
Card-sort and qualitative testing surfaced a clear hierarchy of what users
look at, in what order, and why.

- **Photos are king,** then Tappy content. A photo can be the difference between an immediate pass and spending more time looking at the profile.
- **Looking at subsequent pages signals interest.** If the first page captures interest, users tap into more pages to see more.
- **Bio was unanimously ranked the most important** piece of content. Users use bios to assess personality, affirm authenticity, and gauge a profile's seriousness.
- **Job, school, location, and interests** are also essential. Users look here for dealbreakers and commonalities. Beyond those, opinions varied.
</Section>

<Section stage="Research" eyebrow="Research" title="Men and women look at profiles differently">
A round of A/B tests surfaced a strong gender split. Men preferred seeing
distance first; women preferred bio first. Showing distance first for men
led to revenue lifts of about **0.6%** and a **0.4%** increase in user
satisfaction. The right ordering of content for the right user has a big
impact.
</Section>

<Section stage="Plan" eyebrow="Plan" title="Four design principles">
Before we shaped the solution, the team aligned on four principles to
steer every design decision.
</Section>

<Callouts cols={4} wide>
  <Callout number="1" title="Personalized">
    The solution should cater to individual user preferences and behaviors.
  </Callout>
  <Callout number="2" title="Scalable">
    Develop a system that can efficiently grow and accommodate new business
    or user needs.
  </Callout>
  <Callout number="3" title="Easy to use">
    Seamless for Tinder users, and easy for internal teams to adopt.
  </Callout>
  <Callout number="4" title="Unified">
    Maintain a cohesive visual language across the product.
  </Callout>
</Callouts>

<Section stage="Plan" eyebrow="Plan" title="Four milestones, sequenced for value">
We sequenced the work into four milestones so the team could start
unlocking value early without waiting for the full system.
</Section>

<Callouts cols={2} wide>
  <Callout number="M1" title="Unifying content" tone="outline">
    Audit existing profile content, create profile content components, and
    write Tappy guidelines.
  </Callout>
  <Callout number="M2" title="Building foundations" tone="outline">
    Client-side adoption of the new components, plus enabling
    backend-driven Tappy ordering.
  </Callout>
  <Callout number="M3" title="Centralized tooling" tone="outline">
    Build an internal configuration tool, then use it to run our first
    series of content-ordering tests.
  </Callout>
  <Callout number="M4" title="Rollout and integration" tone="outline">
    Onboard teams to the solution and work with them to fold in and test
    upcoming profile content.
  </Callout>
</Callouts>

<Section stage="Design" eyebrow="Design M1" title="A small set of profile content components">
The first milestone was making everything modular. I audited every kind of
content that lived on Tappy and reduced the surface area to a small set of
reusable components, each with a defined use and styling rules.
</Section>

<Callouts cols={3} wide>
  <Callout title="Content tag">
    For system labels (Active, Online now, Just joined, Recommended). One
    per profile, displayed above the namerow, max 25 characters.
  </Callout>
  <Callout title="Body text">
    For bios and other multi-line text. Truncates with an ellipsis.
  </Callout>
  <Callout title="Text + icon">
    Single-line items with an icon (school, job, distance).
  </Callout>
  <Callout title="Pill labels">
    Short labels in groups or stacks (interests, lifestyle descriptors).
    Configurable colors, optional icons.
  </Callout>
  <Callout title="Image + icon">
    Content with imagery, optional title and body text (Spotify Anthem,
    Relationship Intent).
  </Callout>
  <Callout title="Image grid">
    Up to four images (top Spotify artists, Instagram).
  </Callout>
</Callouts>

<Section stage="Design" eyebrow="Design M1" title="A new information architecture">
Tappy's new architecture splits a profile into three distinct zones, each
with its own purpose. The system labels zone is system-driven, the namerow
is identity, and the profile content zone is what the user authored.
</Section>

<Section stage="Design" eyebrow="Design M1" title="Content maximums on Tappy">
We needed guardrails so a Tappy page could be configured freely without
becoming overwhelming. Each profile element got a "line height" from 1 to 5
based on its actual rendered height, and we capped a Tappy page at **5
lines**. Test participants found a single mixed stack confusing and made
incorrect swipes, so we kept stacking rules clear and let the system
enforce them automatically.
</Section>

<Section stage="Design" eyebrow="Design M3" title="Introducing TaCo, the Tappy Configurator">
With the components and IA in place, the centralizing piece was a tool any
internal team could use to configure Tappy.
</Section>

<Callouts cols={2} wide>
  <Callout number="1" title="Effortless ordering">
    Let teams effortlessly create and manage different Tappy orderings.
  </Callout>
  <Callout number="2" title="Live previews">
    Provide live previews of those orderings as they're configured.
  </Callout>
  <Callout number="3" title="Built-in guardrails">
    Enforce content maximums to prevent profile overcrowding.
  </Callout>
  <Callout number="4" title="Experimentation-native">
    Integrate with our experimentation platform for easy A/B testing.
  </Callout>
</Callouts>
