A mark to wear, in three sizes.

Three directions for a personal logo — italic wordmark, italic JT monogram, and the JT monogram inside an iridescent orb. All built off the same design system.

01 · Best practice

What other designers do

A quick scan of the seven FAANG portfolios you sent. The pattern is mostly wordmark, sometimes mark, rarely avatar.

Wordmark only

Most senior designers ship just this.

Matt Ahrens, Johny Vino, Abdussalam, Aaron James (within his animated frame), Pratibha. Lowest risk, ages well, doesn't try too hard.

Wordmark + symbol

Adds a tiny mark for tight spaces.

Dana Parker (✱ asterisk), Duaa (small leaf glyph). The symbol does the work where the wordmark won't fit — favicon, social avatar, sign-off.

Personal avatar

Warm, but a real commission.

Shuang's watercolor portrait (~200px hero logo). Beautiful and personal but typically illustrated by someone else — worth treating as future polish.

The throughlines: simple, scales to 16×16, works on light and dark, almost always vector. The personal logo is rarely the moment a portfolio wins or loses — clarity and the work below it are. Don't overdesign.

02 · Concepts

Six directions

Each shown big, then on light · dark · brand-color · gradient backgrounds so you can see how it survives. All SVG. All editable.

01 · Wordmark

Italic wordmark

Instrument Serif italic, your full name. The most senior, lowest-risk option — what Matt, Johny, Abdussalam, and most FAANG ICs ship. Descender on "g" stays — it's a wordmark, there's room for it.

Header Footer signature Social bio
02 · Monogram

JT · italic monogram

Italic Instrument Serif JT, tight-kerned, descender intact. The italic slant lets the letters nestle naturally — italic typography is designed for cursive flow. Use at medium-to-large sizes; let the orb (Concept 04) handle small contexts.

Letterhead Resume header Sign-off
03 · Iridescent orb

JT · iridescent orb

Italic JT inside a soft iridescent sphere, echoing the glass-orb-on-dunes inspiration. JT sized to ~50% of the orb so the descender has breathing room inside the circular frame. The most "branded" option — works as favicon and as a resume header badge.

03 · Recommendation

Pick a system, not a mark

A logo isn't one shape — it's a small family of marks for different surfaces. My picks below.

Recommended system

Wordmark + orb, monogram in between

Use Concept 01 (italic wordmark) wherever there's horizontal room — header, footer, resume body, social bio. Use Concept 03 (iridescent orb) as the square mark — favicon, app icon, avatar, resume header badge. Concept 02 (standalone monogram) sits in between — letterhead, sign-offs, anywhere the orb feels too branded but the wordmark is too long.

On the J descending: let it. Italic Instrument Serif's J is designed with the descender — fighting it produces the T-serif-protrusion problem we hit. Italic typography is built for cursive flow; let the letters be themselves. The orb's circular frame contains the descender naturally, which is why it's the right choice for square contexts.

Avatar (Shuang-style): future polish. Worth commissioning a watercolor or line-illustration portrait once the rest of the portfolio is settled — it adds warmth no logo system can. Save it for v0.2.

Production notes once you pick: Convert the chosen mark to outlined paths (not live text) so the SVG doesn't depend on Instrument Serif loading. Export at 16, 32, 180 (apple-touch), 512 (PWA), and SVG. Generate a `favicon.ico`, `favicon.svg`, `apple-touch-icon.png`, `og-image.png` (1200×630 with the lockup centered on the iridescent gradient). I can do all of this once you pick — say the word.
